\begin{abstract}
Scale-Coupled Dynamics (SCD) is a formalised framework in which the only
primitive is a local scale and the geometry is its deformation. This note reports
a revision pass carried out entirely inside the Lean~4 formalisation: thirty-four
new source files and thirty-three new register sections. Its results fall into
four groups.

\emph{Foundations.} The structure carrying axioms A2 and A3 had \emph{never been
constructed} --- every theorem about the scale field was true, clean and
unwitnessed. Two model families are supplied. A periodic scale field is then shown
to be impossible: if the log-scale is defined only modulo a period it is not a
ring element at all, and only its gradient is. Measuring what A2 actually assumes
shows the geometry never sees the log-scale, only its gradient; that A2's content
is \emph{one unit}; and that what it silently adds is \emph{exactness}, which is
the branch of \Athree{} the framework's own gravity sector does not use. The
founding statement \emph{scale $\times$ energy $=$ constant} then needs only a
group homomorphism, the commutative ring having been supplying a second,
independent thing --- a bilinear pairing for the geometry. Seven axioms become
five.

\emph{What can be predicted.} Predictions are informationally disjoint from
inputs, and counts cannot fix a unit; the framework's one dimensionless number is
therefore a \emph{permanent} input, both of its categories being closed. An input
buys a prediction exactly when it appears twice. The escapes to real numbers,
re-invented eight times with no theorem relating any two, are shown to be a single
presheaf plus a \emph{size}; the size adds no freedom, so ``uniform density'' is
precisely the assumption that makes the size and the modulus one parameter.

\emph{The source and the pairing.} No group-valued charge can be a universally
attractive additive source, so the source of the geometry cannot be topological at
all. Separately, the pairing was never chosen: the coupling was computed with a
Euclidean trace while the observables live in a Lorentzian sector, joined by one
theorem whose hypotheses are mutually exclusive. Redoing the curvature identity
with a signature leaves $\kappa=-2(n-1)\Delta$ unchanged, for a structural reason,
but the operator it multiplies is the d'Alembertian: the source law is hyperbolic
and Poisson's equation is its static limit. Scale disturbances then ride the
framework's own null cone, and their effect is quantised by the threshold
spectrum.

\emph{Quantum.} Off the commuting locus the Ricci tensor is \emph{not symmetric},
with $\Ric_{be}-\Ric_{eb}=(n-2)[\sg_b,\sg_e]$, while the connection remains
torsion-free and the scalar curvature does not move at all. Nothing classical
takes a quantum correction at any order; in particular there is no $\hbar G/r^{3}$
correction to the Newtonian potential and no running of $G$. Finally the usual
four-constants--three-dimensions bookkeeping collapses to one grading in which
$\hbar$ and $G$ carry the \emph{same} weight, making ``are they the same
magnitude?'' a well-formed question whose answer is one pure number.

Five claims of the development are withdrawn, and three are kept while their
stated grounds are replaced. All statements are theorems in a Lean~4 development
of 1275 theorems, every one of which passes \lean{\#print axioms} with no axiom
beyond \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound}, and no
\lean{sorry}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, and how to read this}

SCD posits a dimensionless local log-scale $\sg$ over a ring with $n$ commuting
derivations, and derives geometry as the deformation of that scale rather than as
an independent metric structure. This note does not re-derive the framework. It
reports a single revision pass and is written so that every claim can be checked
against a named Lean declaration.

Three words are used with fixed meanings, and they are not decoration.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Claimed} --- proved in the formalisation, with the declaration named.
\item \textbf{Identified} --- an interpretive step the formalisation records as an
      input rather than a consequence.
\item \textbf{Withdrawn} --- a claim previously made in this development and now
      retracted, with the reason.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:open} collects what remains open, including one item this pass
deliberately declined to pursue, and Section~\ref{sec:method} reports the pass's
own error rate, which is the part of a revision note usually omitted.

\part{Foundations re-examined}

\section{A2 had no model, and now has two}
\label{sec:model}

An earlier audit had asked whether the framework's \emph{auxiliary} structures are
ever built from its own carrier. Nobody had asked the same question of the
carriers themselves.

\begin{claimbox}
\lean{ScaleField} --- the structure carrying A2 and A3 --- had \textbf{no term
anywhere in the development}. Not ``only in special cases''. Every theorem about
the scale field was true, clean, and unwitnessed.
\end{claimbox}

The reason is instructive: over a polynomial ring the only units are the constants,
so $\partial\sg=0$ and the scale cannot vary. \lean{ExpPoly.lean} constructs an
exponential-polynomial ring in which $e^{p}$ is a unit, giving the first
non-constant scale field and the first anisotropic directional scale, with an
exact reciprocal at finite amplitude.

\begin{remark}[A second mechanism, and a scope statement]
Exponentiation is not forced. \lean{ExpPoly.dualScaleField} supplies a nilpotent
model that the development had carried for other purposes without recognising it
as a scale field, and \lean{ExpPoly.exp\_not\_first\_order} records the difference:
nilpotent is exact but truncates at finite order. \emph{Both} models sit on the
$\mathbb{R}$ branch of \Athree{} --- and the framework's own analysis says gravity
selects the other branch. So what is closed is ``A2 has no model'', on the branch
the source law does not use.
\end{remark}

\section{There is no periodic scale field}
\label{sec:circle}

The natural next object --- a model that is at once A1--A2 and periodic, so the two
branches of \Athree{} share a carrier --- does not exist.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Circle.no\_scaleField\_with\_uPow}]
If the log-scale is defined only modulo a period then $\sg$ is \textbf{not an
element of the ring}; only its gradient is, because differentiating kills the
ambiguity. In $\mathbb{R}[u,u^{-1}]$ with $d=u\,\partial_u$ the unit $u^{k}$ has a
perfectly good scale gradient --- the constant $k$ --- and for $k\neq0$ no scale
field carries it.
\end{theorem}

So \lean{Axioms.ScaleField}, which has a field $\sg:A$, \emph{is} the $\mathbb{R}$
branch, by construction rather than by modelling accident. The obstruction is
exactness: a non-zero constant is not a derivative, so the gradient is closed and
not exact.

\begin{definition}[\lean{Circle.ScaleGradient}]
A unit $s$, a family $\mathrm{grad}$, and $d\,s=s\cdot\mathrm{grad}$. Exactness
separates the two branches, and \lean{Circle.exact\_iff\_trivial} connects their
carriers.
\end{definition}

\section{What A2 assumes, measured}
\label{sec:gradient}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Gradient.geometry\_congr}]
Every geometric object --- Hessian, Laplacian, squared gradient, connection,
Riemann, Ricci, deformation --- is constructed from the gradient alone, so two
scale fields with the same gradient have the same geometry.
\end{theorem}

\begin{claimbox}
The geometry never sees the log-scale. It sees only its gradient.
\end{claimbox}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Gradient.grad\_unique}, \lean{Gradient.grad\_eq\_logDeriv}]
Given a unit $s$, the equation $d\,s=s\cdot g$ determines $g$. A scale gradient
therefore adds nothing to $s$: \textbf{A2's content is one unit}, the gradient is a
definition, and the log-scale is an \emph{optional potential}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{claimbox}
A2 does not assume ``there is a scale''; it assumes the scale's gradient is
\textbf{exact} --- the $\mathbb{R}$ branch of \Athree{}, chosen silently, and by
the framework's own summary the branch gravity does not select.
\end{claimbox}

\begin{remark}[A cost that was not a cost]
An earlier statement of this result listed the Hessian's symmetry as a casualty of
the reduction. \lean{Gradient.logDeriv\_closed} disproves it: the gradient of
\emph{every} unit is closed, so the symmetry survives with no hypothesis. The claim
is left in place with the correction beside it, because the shape of the mistake
--- assuming a condition must be assumed --- is the one worth remembering.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Torus.torGradient\_not\_isExact}]
Inexactness is not a one-dimensional artefact: the group algebra of
$\mathbb{Z}^{n}$ supplies witnesses in every dimension, with $n=2$ explicit.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The amendment, weighed and declined}

Restating the axioms with the gradient primitive would have made visible which
results need the potential. \lean{Amendment.lean} supplies that information
directly instead.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Amendment.same\_unit\_same\_geometry},
\lean{Amendment.potential\_unique\_up\_to\_fiducial}]
Same unit implies identical geometry: \emph{no} geometric result needs the
potential. And A5's fiducial freedom \emph{is} the choice of potential.
\end{theorem}

The rewrite therefore buys only tidiness and is not made; the axiom text records
the finding instead.

\section{The founding statement needs a group, not a ring}
\label{sec:reduction}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Reduction.logDeriv\_mul}]
$\operatorname{logDeriv}(st)_i=\operatorname{logDeriv}(s)_i+
\operatorname{logDeriv}(t)_i$.
\end{theorem}

The log-derivative is a \emph{group homomorphism} into the additive group of
gradients. This had never been written.

\begin{corollary}[\lean{Reduction.logDeriv\_inv}]
$\operatorname{logDeriv}(s^{-1})=-\operatorname{logDeriv}(s)$ --- which
\textbf{is} A3. That the energy's gradient is minus the scale's is inversion in a
group, not an independent postulate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[\lean{Reduction.ScaleHom}]
A group $G$, an abelian group $M$, and $D\colon G\to M^{n}$ with $D(st)=Ds+Dt$.
Every scale algebra furnishes one. The signature contains \emph{no ring, no
commutativity, no multiplication}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Reduction.gradsq\_add}]
$\operatorname{gradsq}(\sg+\tau)=\operatorname{gradsq}\sg+\operatorname{gradsq}\tau
+2\sum_i\operatorname{sig}\sg_i\cdot\operatorname{sig}\tau_i$.
\end{theorem}

The axiom is linear in the gradient; the geometry is quadratic; and the defect is
exactly a \emph{symmetric bilinear pairing}.

\begin{claimbox}
A commutative ring supplies the homomorphism and the pairing in one move. That is
why it was chosen, and it was never recorded that these are two commitments rather
than one. In a ring, the multiplication defining the unit group and the
multiplication supplying the pairing are the \emph{same operation}, and nothing in
\emph{scale $\times$ energy $=$ constant} requires that.
\end{claimbox}

\section{Axiom consolidation: seven to five}
\label{sec:consolidation}

\begin{proposition}[\lean{Consolidation.A3\_is\_inversion}]
The duality relation is \lean{Units.inv\_mul} and $\varepsilon:=s^{-1}$ is a
definition. A3 names a definition and asserts a group law.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Consolidation.d\_s\_iff\_exact}]
$\bigl(\forall i,\ \partial_i s=s\cdot\operatorname{sig}\sg_i\bigr)
\iff
\bigl(\forall i,\ \operatorname{logDeriv}(s)_i=\operatorname{sig}\sg_i\bigr)$.
\end{theorem}

A2's derivation condition holds exactly when the log-derivative is \emph{exact};
\Afour{} already supplies the unit; and \S\ref{sec:gradient} showed no geometry
needs the potential. The consolidated list is
\[
  \mathrm{A1}\ \cdot\ \mathrm{A2}^{*}\,(\text{one family of units})\ \cdot\
  \mathrm{A5}\ \cdot\ \mathrm{A6}\ \cdot\ \App\ \cdot\ \mathrm{A7}.
\]
The reduction is not claimed to be \emph{forced}; what has changed is that the
potential is now known to be the only surplus.

\part{The observer, resolution, and what can be predicted}

\section{Operator ordering, and the observer screen}
\label{sec:ordering}

The quantitative gravity chain computes over a commutative ring, and carrying its
formulas to a non-commutative ring requires an ordering the commutative
computation does not determine.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Ordering.offDiag\_sub\_rev},
\lean{Ordering.vacuum\_is\_ordering\_dependent}]
The two available orders of the off-diagonal Ricci summand differ by three
commutators of scale gradients per transverse direction --- the same objects the
non-commutative sector calls the correction --- and the difference reaches the
vacuum condition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Ordering.ordering\_irrelevant\_of\_static}]
Under staticity the difference vanishes, so $\gamma=1$, the PPN relation and the
arcsecond figures are unaffected.
\end{theorem}

The observer half of the same question:

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Screen.observer\_preserves\_predictions}]
A quantity constant across admissible worlds is constant across admissible
(world, observer) pairs, because the pairs project into the worlds. \textbf{No
quantity of the world loses its status when the observer joins the
configuration.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Screen.observer\_relative\_is\_modulus}]
For \emph{every} theory admitting one particular resolution --- no constraint on
the world assumed beyond that --- what is \emph{recorded} is a modulus: an input,
not a prediction.
\end{theorem}

\section{Resolution without an order}
\label{sec:resolution}

The standing complaint was that the observer never touches the algebra, because
the observer compares real values while a ring has no order. The diagnosis was too
local: the emergence relation compares reals as well, so \emph{resolution} is what
had to change, not \emph{observer}.

\begin{definition}[\lean{Resolution.Filtration}]
A decreasing family of two-sided ideals; \lean{mem k a} says $a$ is invisible at
resolution $k$, and \lean{Resolved} is its negation. Monotonicity is the emergence
result with the comparison removed.
\end{definition}

\begin{claimbox}
Incompatibility is the framework's own $\operatorname{ad}$ read at a resolution:
\lean{Compatible k a b} $:=$ \lean{mem k (ad a b)}. No projection lattice is
imported --- idempotents had been declined precisely because adopting them imports
the answer.
\end{claimbox}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Layers.compat\_zero}, \lean{Layers.incompat\_one}]
Filtering $2\times2$ matrices by powers of a scalar matrix gives a pair compatible
at resolution $0$ and incompatible at $1$: \textbf{the resolution index does
work}, where before it was decoration.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Layers.resolved\_is\_emergence\_resolved},
\lean{Layers.monomial\_threshold}]
The two notions of ``resolved'' are one, and the thresholds are \emph{computed
from the ring} --- $X^{j}$ has threshold $j$, its degree --- where the emergence
account had taken them as free data.
\end{theorem}

An outcome is then an indistinguishability class
(\lean{Layers.indist\_equivalence}). What is missing is its \emph{weight}: a
probability is a measure on classes, and the framework's only native measure is
counting.

\section{Observer and observed are one type}
\label{sec:vantage}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Vantage.observer\_and\_observed\_are\_one\_type}]
\lean{Resolution} and \lean{Threshold} are both $\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathbb{R}$ ---
each is ``one scale per direction'', which is \Afour{}. The proof is \lean{rfl};
the statement is the content.
\end{theorem}

\lean{Vantage.every\_vantage\_observes\_itself} and
\lean{Vantage.self\_observation\_is\_exact} are reflexivity and antisymmetry, so
the vantage space is \textbf{a poset whose order is observation}.

\begin{claimbox}
The framework has no position space --- its own axiom text says directions are
indices at a point, not positions --- so ``the relation between the observer and
spacetime'' has no second term. What exists is a vantage space, and an observer is
a point of it together with a commitment.
\end{claimbox}

Content is \emph{covariant} in the vantage
(\lean{Vantage.record\_mono\_in\_resolution}, new here) and \emph{contravariant} in
the commitment, so it is a bifunctor
(\lean{Vantage.the\_two\_variances\_commute}). An observer committing no direction
records everything; committing all of them is detachment, and it records least ---
an uncertainty-shaped statement obtained by unfolding an intersection, and not
claimed to be more.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Vantage.algebra\_record\_is\_observer\_record}]
Given a filtration \emph{per direction}, \lean{Vantage.place} sends a ring element
to one real per direction --- its threshold there --- and the algebraic resolved
set \emph{is} the observer's record. This is the first map from the algebra into
the observer's space.
\end{theorem}

The cost is stated: \lean{place} needs $n$ filtrations, and a filtration is
postulated, so this multiplies an unwitnessed structure rather than discharging it.

\section{What can be predicted, and what cannot}
\label{sec:predict}

\subsection{Equal spacing dies against the data}

A filtration by powers of one element spaces its thresholds evenly.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Spacing.thresholds\_not\_power\_spaced}]
The measured spectrum's gaps are not evenly spaced, so no such filtration
reproduces them.
\end{theorem}

\lean{Spacing.gapList\_shift\_invariant} and \lean{Spacing.gapList\_rescale} record
what the gap list does and does not see, and \lean{Spacing.unique\_pure\_number}
isolates the one number that survives.

\subsection{Predictions carry no information about inputs}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Information.predicted\_cannot\_determine\_modulus}]
If $f$ is constant across a theory's solutions and $g$ is not, then $g$ is not a
function of $f$: the two solutions that disagree about $g$ agree about $f$.
\end{theorem}

Trivial as a proof and not as a statement --- it retires an entire class of routes.
A prediction can never fix a free number.

\subsection{And counts cannot fix a unit}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Conversion.counts\_cannot\_fix\_a\_unit},
\lean{Conversion.threshold\_not\_a\_function\_of\_charge}]
A count is invariant under the rescaling that moves the unit, so no count
determines it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{claimbox}
$\Delta\rho$ is not an open problem. It is a \textbf{permanent input} --- unless a
quantity that is neither a prediction nor a count is introduced, and the framework
has no third category.
\end{claimbox}

\subsection{But an input buys a prediction when it appears twice}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Overdetermination.one\_number\_two\_relations}]
A free number occurring in one relation is absorbed by it and predicts nothing; in
two, it is fixed by one measurement and predicts the other.
\end{theorem}

The question therefore becomes a \emph{count of appearances}, and
\lean{Overdetermination.coupling\_from\_rate\_and\_density} exhibits the shape a
second appearance would have to take.

\subsection{The rate's weight}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{RateWeight.gauge\_conversion\_moves},
\lean{RateWeight.prediction\_at\_unit\_factor}]
The dissipation rate is \textbf{weight zero}, by two independent computations that
agree.
\end{theorem}

This kills one branch of the remaining dimensionful question and makes the gap
exact.

\section{The cosmological sector shares no types with the algebra}
\label{sec:disconnect}

\begin{claimbox}
The cosmological files import only Mathlib; the log-scale does not occur in them;
the scanning account takes its coupling, density and time as free real parameters
and mentions the scale algebra nowhere --- while the file where
$\kappa=-2(n-1)\Delta$ lives does import the algebra.
\end{claimbox}

\lean{Disconnect.factorisation\_is\_generic} and
\lean{Disconnect.factorisation\_has\_failing\_instances} make it precise. There is
no unit conversion to compute: the cosmological time variable is not the scale axis
in other units, it is an unconnected real variable. \emph{The bookkeeping is not
hard --- it is not yet a question.}

\begin{remark}[Withdrawn]
An earlier claim of this pass --- that a bridge construction makes the scanning
hypothesis a \emph{theorem} of the index law
(\lean{Bridge.factorsThroughCount\_of\_indexResponse}) --- is withdrawn. The
theorem stands at the pair the bridge constructs; what was written around it,
identifying that pair with the cosmological one, does not.
\lean{Observations.lean} keeps the two remaining identifications as explicit
hypotheses carried visibly downstream. That also caught a trap: an identification
written as a \emph{structure} with the observed number as a field is trivially
inhabited, since one can always name a real equal to a formula. It is written as a
\lean{Prop} parameterised by the observed number instead.
\end{remark}

\section{The unstated primitive: presheaves, and a size}
\label{sec:content}

Counting what the disconnection is a case of: at the time of the audit,
\textbf{59 of 107 files never mentioned the scale algebra} (now 66 of 125), and
the escape to real numbers had been re-invented \textbf{eight times}, with
\emph{no theorem relating any two}.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Content.pullback\_eq\_emergence}]
Three of the framework's own monotonicity results \emph{are} functor laws. The
algebraic sector and the real-valued threshold sector are \textbf{one presheaf
described twice}; and the observer is a presheaf of the opposite variance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Size.emergence\_is\_ofThreshold},
\lean{Size.uniform\_density\_is\_a\_restriction}]
Thresholds and content are two descriptions of one datum, so the count of
independent escapes was too high. What the rest are is not content but a
\emph{size} --- a measure of content --- and its linearity is a restriction, not a
shape it must have.
\end{theorem}

\begin{claimbox}
G4's missing weight and the cosmological disconnection are \textbf{one gap},
approached from two sides, and it is the \emph{size}.
\end{claimbox}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Valuation.observer\_counting\_law},
\lean{Valuation.modularity\_is\_a\_restriction}]
The framework's own union law for observers, fed to a \emph{modular} size, yields
inclusion--exclusion on the observer lattice. Nothing picks the weight; nothing
resists it either. Monotonicity alone is far too little --- ``one if anything is
there'' is monotone.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{And the size adds no freedom}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{OneParameter.affine\_size\_determined},
\lean{OneParameter.origin\_is\_a\_fiducial\_shift},
\lean{OneParameter.size\_adds\_no\_freedom}]
A uniform size is $\rho(t-t_0)$; that is all of it; its origin is A5's rather than
an observable; and its slope \emph{is} the density already counted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{claimbox}
The size adds no freedom. \emph{Uniform density is not an assumption beside
$\Delta\rho$: it is the assumption that makes them one parameter.}
\end{claimbox}

The register had carried uniform density among its assumed items from the beginning
without saying what it buys. This is what it buys.

\part{The source and the pairing}

\section{The source of the geometry cannot be a topological charge}
\label{sec:topology}

\App{} reads $\Delta\sg=\Delta\cdot\nu$ and was presented as an \emph{index} law
with $\nu$ topological. Three candidate integers had been tabulated.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Codimension2.two\_valued\_source\_is\_trivial}]
An additive map from a two-element group to $\mathbb{R}$ is identically zero, since
$a^{2}=1$ forces $2f(a)=0$. The projective order parameter's line defects carry
exactly that charge, and \lean{Codimension2.odd\_pairs\_cancel} is the framework's
own selection rule.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{DegreeSource.additive\_nonneg\_on\_group\_is\_trivial}]
On a \emph{group}, an additive $f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}$ with $f\geq0$ is
identically zero: $f(1)=0$, then $f(a)+f(a^{-1})=0$ with both terms non-negative.
\end{theorem}

One theorem covers both branches --- the two-valued charge dies at $a=a^{-1}$, the
signed degree at $f(k)+f(-k)=0$ --- and it never needed to know the codimension.
Additivity is what makes a source an index; non-negativity is what universal
attraction requires.

\begin{claimbox}
No group-valued charge can be a universally attractive additive source. Every
topological charge here is group-valued, so the source of the geometry cannot be
topological at all. The question ``which codimension?'' was malformed on both
branches: it has an answer, and the answer is neither.
\end{claimbox}

The result is sharp rather than over-strong.
\lean{DegreeSource.monoid\_source\_exists} gives an additive, non-negative,
non-zero map on $\mathbb{N}$: it is \emph{inverses} that do the killing, and a
threshold count survives because content is a cardinality with no anti-content. Two
external checks: real gravity's source is not topological and mass is non-negative;
and electric charge \emph{is} signed and \emph{does} source an interaction, which
the theorem permits because that interaction is not universally attractive.

\begin{remark}[And the reproduced coefficient means nothing]
\lean{DegreeSource.degree\_gives\_the\_same\_kappa} is the original derivation
\emph{applied}, not reproved: its $\Delta$ was already an arbitrary ring element
and the derivation used no property of it, so any constant gives the same formula
with itself in the slot. ``Does the degree version give the same $\kappa$?'' was a
malformed question. What is touched is \App{}'s standing: its authority came from
$\nu$ being a topological index. \emph{\App{} keeps its consequences and loses its
index reading.}
\end{remark}

\section{The pairing was never chosen: two metrics}
\label{sec:twometrics}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \lean{Conformal.RscBare} $=\sum_b\Ric_{bb}$, called in its own documentation
      the \emph{$\delta$-trace} of Ricci, on the metric $e^{2\sg}\delta$. This is
      where $\kappa=-2(n-1)\Delta$ is derived, so \textbf{$\kappa$ is a Euclidean
      result}.
\item \lean{Diagonal.Ric} on a metric carrying the signature as a parameter with
      $\eta^{2}=1$. The development's own chain table runs the \emph{entire}
      gravity chain through this sector, and the key cancellation requires
      $\eta_t\eta_r=-1$.
\end{itemize}
